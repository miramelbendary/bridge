# Sample teaching notes: localisation for Arabic-speaking audiences

> Sample material written for the BRIDGE demo. Replace with lecturer-approved course notes before
> any real use.

## Translation brief and target audience

Before translating, establish the purpose, target audience, channel and register. A marketing text
for Egyptian consumers on social media calls for different choices than a formal brochure for the
whole Arab region. When the brief is unclear, ask the client.

## Clarification requests

Good clarification questions are specific, grouped, and propose options: "Should the slogan stay in
English or be adapted into Arabic? Option A… Option B…". Avoid sending many separate messages.

## Preservation of meaning

Translate the message and intended effect, not word by word. Check that claims, numbers, product
names and calls to action are preserved exactly.

## Terminology and glossaries

Keep product and brand terms consistent. Agree a glossary with the client and follow it. Note when a
loanword (for example a technical term) is more natural to the audience than a literal coinage.

## Cultural adaptation and localisation

Adapt idioms, humour, imagery, examples, units, currency and dates to the target culture. Egyptian
Arabic (العامية المصرية) may suit informal social-media campaigns; Modern Standard Arabic
(الفصحى) suits formal or pan-Arab material. Check that references are appropriate and respectful.

## Grammar, punctuation and right-to-left layout

Arabic uses its own punctuation (، ؛ ؟). Check agreement, spelling of hamza and taa marbuta, and that
numbers and Latin-script brand names display correctly in right-to-left text.

## Handling client feedback

Separate preferential changes from errors. Accept valid corrections, explain your choices
politely when you disagree, and document agreed changes in the glossary.

## Professional ethics in translation

Do not add or remove claims; flag potentially misleading or culturally offensive content to the
client instead of silently changing it. Respect confidentiality.
