# Sample teaching notes: crisis communication and verification

> Sample material written for the BRIDGE demo. Replace with lecturer-approved course notes before
> any real use.

## Crisis management basics

In the first hours of a crisis: establish the facts, assemble the people who can decide, agree a
holding statement, and choose who speaks. Speed matters, but accuracy matters more.

## Information verification

Verify before you amplify. Check the original source, date and location of a video; look for
signs of editing; compare with internal records and eyewitnesses. Distinguish confirmed facts,
unconfirmed claims and what you do not know yet.

## Holding statements and public statements

A holding statement acknowledges the situation, shows concern, says what is being done and when
more information will follow — without speculating. A full statement adds verified facts, takes
responsibility where appropriate, and states concrete next steps.

## Responding to journalists

Be accurate, consistent and timely. Do not say "no comment"; say what you can confirm and when you
will know more. Never speculate or blame individuals publicly before facts are established.

## Misinformation and not amplifying false claims

Repeating a false claim can spread it further. Lead with the true information ("truth sandwich":
fact, brief mention of the false claim, fact again). Correct through channels where the
audience actually is.

## Reputation management and recovery

Recovery depends on consistent behaviour after the crisis: corrective actions, transparency about
progress, and listening to stakeholders. Monitor sentiment, but remember that online reactions are
not the whole public.

## Media literacy

Understand how platforms amplify content, how engagement differs from reach, and how to read
audience reactions critically.

## Professional ethics in communication

Do not mislead, do not hide material facts, respect privacy of employees and the public, and keep
records of what was said and when.
